Hope and fear, in their quiet symmetry, are not opposites. They are twins born of a single impulse: the mind reaching outward, tethering itself to the unshaped future, grasping at things that never belonged to it.

As the Stoic Hecato of Rhodes understood, both projections share the same weight. They are equally heavy shadows cast over the only ground we actually possess.

> *"Both are the enemy of this present moment that you are actually in."*

### Conclusion

To hope is to quietly invite want. The longer the yearning lingers, the deeper the worry takes root. In that quiet accumulation, the distance between what is and what is desired, lies the only true peril.
