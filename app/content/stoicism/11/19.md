In *Enchiridion*, 53, Epictetus invokes three ancient fragments. Together, they trace a quiet progression: from tolerance, to flexibility, and finally—inevitably—to acceptance.

Today’s entry in :daily-stoic offers a practical bridge, translating that ancient sequence into the texture of our daily lives.

For the Christian, this terrain feels familiar. Faith in a higher power anchors the work of surrender. Yet lived experience often blurs our vision; we stand too close to the friction to see its shape. The remedy is simple, if not easy: step back. Widen the frame, even by a fraction, until perspective returns.

### Conclusion

Consider, for a moment, a quiet premise.

Imagine that every event—welcome or unwelcome—arrives not by accident, but by design. Appointed specifically for you. You cannot see the horizon, nor decipher the ending. You only know there is intent behind the unfolding.

Knowing that, would you still choose to fight?
